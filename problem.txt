Problem Statement
Gender-based violence (GBV) in South Africa is a pervasive and escalating crisis:
According to 1000 Women 1 Voice, A shocking 35.5% of South African women (over 7.8 million) have experienced physical or sexual violence in their lifetime
Nearly 10% have suffered sexual violence, and over 33% have experienced physical violence 

In the latest reporting period (April 2022–March 2023), according to Amnesty International, the South African Police Service documented over 42,780 reported rapes,
amounting to 116 rape cases per day

Femicide rates in South Africa are roughly five times higher than the global average, with intimate partner violence claiming approximately
5.5 women per 100,000 between 2020 and 2021 - SSLGDC - UN Women.

Between July and September 2024 alone, 957 women were murdered, 1,567 survived attempted murders, and over 14,300 were assaulted, including 10,191 reported cases of rape - SAnews.

Taken together, these numbers represent not just statistics—but millions of lived experiences marked by fear, trauma, and systemic failure.

Why “SafeSpace” Matters

These figures reflect not only the scale of abuse, but also gaps in reporting, support access, and survivor empowerment. Many survivors face barriers like stigma,
fear, and institutional inadequacy, often resulting in underreporting and inadequate support 

SafeSpace can bridge these critical gaps by delivering:
- Safe, accessible reporting channels
- Trauma-informed design that prioritizes privacy and psychological safety
- Resources and support tailored to survivors’ needs
- Data-driven insights to help NGOs, policymakers, and communities respond effectively
